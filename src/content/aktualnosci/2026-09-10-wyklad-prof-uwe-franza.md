  You are cordially invited on Tuesday **15 September** at **13:00** to room **WS** for an open lecture by Prof. **Uwe Franz** (Université Marie & Louis Pasteur, Besançon):

  *Who was Seki Takakazu?*

  The lecture is part of the IDUB programme.

  ## Abstract

  Seki Takakazu, also known as Seki Kowa, lived in the Edo period, when Japan isolated itself from the rest of the world. He discovered the Bernoulli numbers independently of Jacob Bernoulli, introduced determinants before Leibniz, and laid the foundations for the development of the Japanese mathematics of the 17th and 18th century known as Wasan. In my talk I will summarize what we know about his life and present several of his most important results.

  ## About the speaker

  Uwe Franz is a professor of mathematics at Université Marie & Louis Pasteur. His research focuses on compact quantum groups, non-commutative probability and non-commutative stochastic calculus. He has authored 80 research papers and 3 monographs, supervised seven PhD students, led numerous grant projects and organised many international conferences. He spent two years in Japan as a Marie Curie fellow and was a fellow of the Alfried Krupp Wissenschaftskolleg in Greifswald. He is fluent in German, English, French and Japanese.
