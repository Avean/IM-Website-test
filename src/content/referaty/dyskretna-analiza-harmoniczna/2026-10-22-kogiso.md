The functional equations for the Riemann zeta function have long been known to admit two classical proofs. One is based on the Mellin transform and an integral representation of the zeta function, while the other relies on Jacobi's theta function. Although these approaches are quite different, they are in fact two manifestations of the same underlying principle: The Poisson summation formula and the self-duality of the Fourier transform. The main theme of this lecture is how this remarkable phenomenon extends to higher dimensions. A successful framework for such a generalization is provided by the theory of prehomogeneous vector spaces, introduced by Mikio Sato. In this talk, I will discuss local functional equations associated with these spaces, the polynomials that satisfy them, and their intimate connections with representation theory, harmonic analysis and number theory, and combinatorics.
